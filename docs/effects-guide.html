Jutsu effects, frame by frame

A Procreate tutor for the smoke, fire, water and lightning in your jutsu app. Every number, colour, brush and frame is here, and it's written for someone new to animation.

Start here

Work through this page in order the first time. The first half teaches the ideas that make anime effects feel smooth. The second half is a recipe for each effect, with a frame-by-frame chart.

A realistic plan

  1. Day 1: read up to "How to draw any effect", set up Procreate, and do Drill 1 (the bouncing ball). About 1 hour.
  2. Day 2: Drills 2 and 3: one smoke puff and a looping candle flame. About 2 hours.
  3. Days 3 to 5: Effect 1, the smoke poof. This is the one the app needs most, and it teaches everything the others use.
  4. Then: fireball, water splash, sparks, and the sword trails if you want them.

Each effect is 10 to 12 drawings. Expect your first one to take an evening or two. The second takes half as long.

Why it felt too fast

When you raised the frame rate above 5 fps and it felt too fast, the speed setting wasn't the real problem. The motion needed more drawings between the big moments, and it needed to hold on the important ones. Here's how anime handles it.

Anime runs at 24 fps but draws "on twos"

TV anime plays at 24 frames per second, but most drawings stay on screen for 2 frames. That's called animating "on twos": 12 new drawings per second. Calm moments are often "on threes" (8 drawings per second). Only the fastest hits are "on ones" (24 per second).

So set Procreate to 24 fps, then use Hold duration to keep each drawing on screen for 2 or 3 frames. You get anime timing without drawing 24 pictures per second.

The two settings that matter

  • Frames per second: 24. Leave it there for every effect in this guide.
  • Hold duration on each drawing: 1, 2 or 3 frames. The charts below tell you which, drawing by drawing.

If you'd rather not use holds at first, set 12 fps and give every drawing 1 frame. That's the same speed as "on twos". Add holds later, for the peaks.

Timing and spacing

Timing is how many frames something takes. Spacing is how far it moves or grows between one drawing and the next. A sloppy, jumpy feel almost always comes from spacing, not timing:

  • Even spacing (the same change every drawing) looks robotic.
  • Ease out: big changes first, then smaller and smaller. Explosions, puffs and splashes move this way: fast burst, slow settle.
  • Ease in: small changes first, then bigger. A falling droplet does this, speeding up with gravity.

Picture a smoke puff growing from 10% to 80% of the canvas over five drawings. With even spacing it reads 10, 24, 38, 52, 66, 80. With an ease out it reads 10, 45, 65, 74, 78, 80, which is how the anime does it.

Anticipation, hit, hold, follow-through

Every effect in this guide has the same four beats. The charts colour them:

  • Wind-up: a small, tight shape, sometimes squashed, held a moment
  • Hit: the fast burst, on ones or twos
  • Peak: the biggest drawing, slightly too big, held
  • Hold: churning and rolling while it stays big
  • Break-up: splitting into pieces, on threes

Holding the peak is what makes an effect feel powerful instead of fast. The eye needs about 3 to 6 frames to read a big shape.

Procreate set-up

New canvas

  1. In the Gallery tap +, then the new custom size icon at the top right.
  2. Dimensions: width 1024, height 1024, units pixels. DPI: 132 (it makes no difference on screen; any value works).
  3. Colour Profile: RGB, sRGB IEC61966-2.1. Not Display P3: P3 colours shift when they reach a web browser.
  4. Name it, for example smoke-poof, and tap Create.

Make the canvas once, then duplicate it from the Gallery for each new effect, so every file has the same settings.

Turn on Animation Assist

Actions (spanner) → Canvas → Animation Assist. A timeline appears at the bottom. Tap Settings in it:

SettingValueWhy
PlaybackLoopSo you see the effect over and over while you judge it.
Frames per second24Anime speed. Holds control how long each drawing stays.
Onion skin frames2Shows the 2 drawings before and after, faintly. More than that is clutter.
Onion skin opacity35%Enough to trace motion, not enough to confuse lines.
Blend primary frameOffKeeps the drawing you're working on solid.
Colour secondary framesOnTints previous frames red and next frames green, so you can tell them apart.

To set a drawing's hold: tap its thumbnail in the timeline, then drag Hold duration. A hold of 2 shows the drawing for 2 frames.

Layers: one group per drawing

In Animation Assist, every top-level layer or group is one frame. Use a group per drawing, named 01, 02 and so on, with the same layers inside every time:

01 (group)
  lines
  highlight  (clipping mask)
  shadow     (clipping mask)
  flat colour

  • Make a group: select layers with a swipe right on each, then tap Group.
  • Make a clipping mask: tap a layer's thumbnail, choose Clipping Mask. It only paints where the layer below has colour, so shadows never spill outside the shape.
  • Do rough sketches in a separate group named rough, hidden or deleted before export.

Guides and helpers

  • Find the centre: Actions → Canvas → Drawing Guide, then Edit Drawing Guide → 2D Grid, grid size 128 px. The centre is where your hands are in the app. Every effect starts from it.
  • Reference: Actions → Canvas → Reference opens a small floating window. Choose Image and load a paused screenshot from an episode to study the shapes and timing. Don't trace it.
  • Flip to check: Actions → Canvas → Flip Canvas Horizontally. Mistakes in shape and balance jump out when the image is mirrored.
  • Gestures: two-finger tap to undo, three-finger tap to redo, four-finger tap to hide the interface, pinch quickly to fit the canvas on screen.

Make a palette per effect

Open Colours → Palettes, tap +, Create new palette and name it after the effect. Type each colour's hex code in the Value tab (the Hex field), then tap an empty square in your palette to save it. Every effect below lists its colours; tap a hex code on this page to copy it.

Rules of anime effects

Shapes

  • Smoke is clusters of round billows, like stacked "C" curves. Big billows in front, small ones behind.
  • Fire is teardrops and S-curves that taper to points, always reaching up or outward.
  • Water is arcs and crowns: a lifted sheet with a rim of droplets, and curls at the tips.
  • Lightning is zigzags with short branches. Straight segments, sharp corners, never wobbly.

Line weight

Press harder on the outside contour and lighter on the inside lines. Taper the ends of every inner line. One weight everywhere looks like a colouring book.

Colour: flat, shadow, highlight

Cel shading uses three tones: the flat colour, one shadow and one highlight. All shadows sit on the same side. In this guide the light comes from the top left, so shadows go bottom right on every billow, flame and splash.

Disappear by breaking apart

Anime effects rarely fade with opacity. They break up: holes open in the middle, edges split into smaller pieces, and pieces shrink to nothing. That one habit makes your effects look like the show.

Keep the volume

Between two drawings, a shape can change but shouldn't randomly gain or lose size. Watch the onion skin: if the outline jumps in or out without reason, that's what reads as sloppy.

Overlap the action

Not every part moves at once. The big billow leads and the small ones follow a drawing later. Sparks fly out after the burst, not with it.

How to draw any effect

Professional effects animators work in passes, never one finished drawing after another. Follow this order for every effect:

  1. Keys. In the rough group, sketch only the wind-up, the peak and the last drawing. Three drawings, loose and fast, in light blue. Play them. Does the size and direction feel right?
  2. Breakdowns. Add the drawings halfway between the keys, still rough. Now you have about half the drawings.
  3. In-betweens. Fill the rest. Use the onion skin: each in-between sits between its neighbours, closer to whichever end the chart's spacing says.
  4. Play and fix the timing with holds. Change the rough first; it's cheap.
  5. Clean lines on the lines layer in each drawing's group, Studio Pen, one confident stroke per curve.
  6. Flat colour with ColorDrop on flat colour.
  7. Shadows and highlights on their clipping masks.
  8. Final play at 24 fps with holds, on a mirrored canvas too. Then export.

To start a new drawing from the last one, swipe left on its group and tap Duplicate, then change it. That keeps the volume steady during the hold phase.

Three warm-up drills

Drill 1: the bouncing ball about 1 hour

The classic first exercise, and the fastest way to feel spacing. On a 1024 × 1024 canvas, draw a 120 px ball falling from the top and bouncing on a line near the bottom.

  • Seven drawings down, at 24 fps with each held 2 frames.
  • Spacing: close together at the top, further apart as it falls (gravity, ease in).
  • On contact: squash it wide and flat for one drawing, then stretch it tall as it leaves.
  • Play it at 24 fps with holds of 2. Then try holds of 1 and 3 and feel the difference.

Drill 2: one smoke puff about 1 hour

One round billow appearing and dissolving, in 8 drawings: tiny (1), burst to half size (2), full size, slightly too big (3), settle (4), hold with a small wobble (5), a hole opens (6), splits into three pieces (7), pieces shrink away (8). Use the smoke palette below and holds of 2, 1, 2, 3, 2, 2, 3, 3.

Drill 3: a candle flame loop about 1 hour

A single flame flickering in a loop: 6 drawings, each held 2 frames. The tip sways left, centre, right, centre, splits once, rejoins. The last drawing must flow into the first. Colour it with the fire palette in bands: red outside, then orange, then yellow, then a white core.

Effect 1: Smoke poof

What it is: the "POOF" of smoke that every summoning and clone jutsu appears in. In the app it plays for Summoning Jutsu, Clone Jutsu, Substitution Jutsu and Fanged Pursuit.

Study: pause any summoning in Naruto, for example Kakashi's ninja hounds or Naruto summoning a toad, and step through it. Notice how fast it bursts and how long the billows hang before breaking up.

DrawingHoldWhat to draw
12A tight ball of 3 billows at the centre, about 15% of the canvas. Slightly squashed.
21Burst to 50%. Billows pushing outward, outline lumpy. Optional: 4 short straight speed lines radiating out.
3180% of the canvas, rounder. This is the overshoot.
43The peak: 75%, settled. 7 to 9 clear billows, big in front, small behind. Full shadows.
52Same size; billows roll a little clockwise. Duplicate 4 and nudge each billow.
62Holes open between billows near the middle. The outer billows start to separate.
72Splits into 5 or 6 puffs drifting outward and slightly up.
83Puffs at 70% of their size, further out.
93Puffs at 40%. Two have vanished.
103Two or three tiny puffs left.

Technique

  • Draw each billow as a "C" curve with Studio Pen, outer contour heavier. QuickShape helps: draw a circle and hold to snap it, then erase the parts hidden behind other billows.
  • Shadow (Mist) along the bottom-right inside each billow, as a crescent. Deep shadow (Storm Grey) only in the folds where billows meet.
  • No highlight colour needed: the white flat is the highlight.
  • Add 2 or 3 inner lines per billow, thin and tapered, to show its roundness.

Common mistakes

  • All billows the same size, which reads as bubble wrap. Vary them: three big, four medium, a few small.
  • Fading with opacity instead of breaking up.
  • The peak not held long enough. If it feels weak, add 1 frame to drawing 4.

Extras: three single puffs of different sizes on separate layers, not animated, for the app to scatter.

Effect 2: Fireball

What it is: the Uchiha Fire Style: Fireball, a huge rolling ball of fire. In the app it plays for Fireball, Phoenix Flower, Dragon Flame and Dragon Flame Bomb.

Study: Sasuke's or Itachi's fireballs. Notice the colour bands (dark red edge, orange, yellow, white core) and how the ball keeps churning while it holds.

DrawingHoldWhat to draw
12A small bright ball at the centre, 10%: white core, yellow edge.
21Squashed slightly wider: the wind-up.
3135%. Bands appear: yellow inside, orange outside. Five short tongues start.
4160%. Tongues longer, curling outward.
53The peak: 85%. All bands visible: red edge, orange, yellow, white core. Eight to ten tongues around the rim, longest at the top.
62Churning: the inner bands shift a quarter turn, tongues change places. Same size.
72Churn again. The top starts to rise.
82Churn; the whole ball drifts up about 5%.
92Breaks into four or five big tongues rising separately.
103Tongues thinner and further apart. Embers (tiny orange dots) fly out.
113Small tongues and embers, plus two wisps of Ash smoke.
123A few small tongues, embers and smoke wisps.

Technique

  • Fire usually has no outline. Its shape comes from the colour bands. Draw each band as a closed shape and fill it with ColorDrop, outside in: Ember, then Naruto Orange, then Sun Yellow, then White Heat.
  • Shadow: Deep Ember on the bottom-right edge, and at the tips of the longest tongues.
  • Glow (optional): a Soft Brush pass of Sun Yellow on a glow layer set to Add, at 40%.
  • Tongues are S-curves that taper to a sharp point. Draw each in one stroke.

Common mistakes

  • Bands as neat circles. Make them wavy and uneven, thicker at the top.
  • Too much white: keep the core small, or the fireball looks like a lightbulb.
  • Fire falling down. Fire always rises: every piece drifts up as it breaks apart.

Extras: five single flame tongues, flame_a.png to flame_e.png.

Effect 3: Water splash

What it is: a crown of water bursting up from below the hands. In the app it plays for Water Trumpet and Water Shark Bomb.

Study: any water jutsu in Naruto, and the water in Demon Slayer, which takes its style from Hokusai's woodblock waves: thick dark outlines, white curling foam, and claw-shaped crest tips.

DrawingHoldWhat to draw
12A bulge of water just below the centre, 20% wide.
21A column shoots up to 40%.
31The column opens into a crown, 70%. The first droplets leave the rim.
43The peak: a full crown, 80%, with 6 to 8 claw-shaped tips and droplets flung outward in arcs.
52The crown tips curl over. Droplets fly further out, still rising slightly.
62The crown starts to fall. Droplets turn downward. Their spacing gets wider each drawing (gravity).
72The crown collapses into a ring of water.
83A ring of foam and splashing drops.
93Foam pieces and a few drops.
103The last drops.

Technique

  • Outline with Technical Pen in Abyss, 6 to 8%. Water lines are smooth and even.
  • Flat colour Leaf Pool Blue. Shadow Deep on the bottom-right side of every shape.
  • One curved white Foam stripe on each big shape, following its curve. That one stripe makes it read as water.
  • Droplets are teardrops pointing back along their path.
  • For the Demon Slayer look, add white swirls and dots of foam along the crest, and make each tip a small curl.

Common mistakes

  • Droplets falling at an even speed. Space them wider as they fall.
  • Too many tiny droplets. Fewer, bigger drops read better.

Extras: four single droplets, drop_a.png to drop_d.png.

File names: water_01.png to water_10.png.

Effect 4: Chidori sparks optional

What it is: the crackling electric arcs around Chidori. The app already draws good lightning in code, so this only adds a hand-drawn layer on top.

The big difference: lightning is not in-betweened. Every drawing is a completely new arrangement. Smoothness would make it look slow and fake.

A loop of about 8 frames, a third of a second, that the app repeats.

  • Canvas: 512 × 512 px, same colour profile.
  • Each drawing: 5 to 8 bolts from the centre outward, each a zigzag of 4 to 6 straight segments with 1 or 2 short branches. Monoline, 4%, Core White.
  • Duplicate the bolts layer, move it under, set it to Chakra Light, and make it a little thicker as the edge.
  • Glow: Soft Brush, Chidori Blue, on a layer set to Add, 50%.
  • Drawings 3 and 6 are the brightest, with more bolts.

File names: spark_01.png to spark_06.png.

Bonus: sword trails, Demon Slayer style optional

Ufotable's Demon Slayer draws breathing techniques as ribbons that follow the sword. Water Breathing looks like an ukiyo-e wave; Flame Breathing is a ribbon of fire. These would suit a future slash effect in the app, or a cast that sweeps across the panel. Make them only after the four effects above.

Water Breathing ribbon

  • Canvas: 2048 × 1024 px (wide), same colour profile.
  • Path: the ribbon sweeps in an arc from the left edge to the right, like a sword slash, wide in the middle and thin at both ends.
  • Drawings 1 to 4: the ribbon's leading tip races along the arc. Each drawing it covers about a quarter more of the path. Fast, on ones.
  • Drawing 5, held: the full ribbon. Along its top edge, a scalloped crest of claw-shaped foam tips. At both ends, a tight spiral curl.
  • Drawings 6 and 7: the water keeps flowing: shift the inner white flow lines along the ribbon each drawing, and keep the outline still.
  • Drawings 8 to 12: the ribbon breaks from its tail end into curling pieces and foam dots.
  • Lines: Technical Pen in Night Ink, thick (8%) outside and thin (3%) for the inner flow lines in Foam.

Flame Breathing ribbon

  • Same canvas, path and timing chart as the water ribbon.
  • The ribbon is a sweep of fire: along its outer edge, flame tongues lick backward, away from the direction of the slash.
  • No outline. Bands from the outside in: Crimson, Blaze, Gold, then a thin Flash line through the centre.
  • On the hold drawings, the tongues flicker: redraw them each time while the ribbon's body stays put.
  • The break-up leaves rising tongues and embers, like the fireball.

File names: trail-water_01.png onward, trail-flame_01.png onward.

Export and hand-off

  1. Delete or hide the rough group. Hide the Background layer, so the export is transparent.
  2. Check the drawing order in the timeline, and play it once more at 24 fps with your holds.
  3. Animated version: Actions → Share → Animated PNG, choose Max resolution and keep 24 fps. This keeps your hold timings, so the app plays it exactly as you timed it.
  4. Separate frames: Actions → Share → PNG Files. This exports every group as its own PNG, numbered in order. Rename them as each effect lists (smoke_01.png and so on).
  5. Export extras (single puffs, flames, droplets) from their own canvas the same way.
  6. Put everything in OneDrive → Desktop/Projects/jutsu-art/, a subfolder per effect, then tell me. I'll make sprite sheets and wire them into the app.

Checklist before you send

  • 1024 × 1024 px (512 for sparks, 2048 × 1024 for trails), sRGB.
  • Transparent background, nothing touching the edges.
  • The effect grows from the centre of the canvas in every drawing.
  • No text, no Japanese characters, nothing traced from the show.
  • Both the Animated PNG and the numbered PNG files.
